What should a sole proprietor put on a U.S. invoice?

For an ordinary U.S. sole proprietor’s domestic service invoice, there is no single federal invoice template: IRS guidance focuses on records that clearly show income and expenses. Include the parties, dates, work, amounts and payment terms, then check any state, industry and customer requirements.

Source: IRS — What kind of records should I keep? · IRS — Publication 583: recordkeeping · New York — Recordkeeping for sales tax vendors

A useful checklist, not one nationwide legal minimum

Sales tax and invoice-detail rules can vary by jurisdiction. For example, New York sales-tax vendors must identify taxable and nontaxable items and show sales tax separately.

Source: New York — Recordkeeping for sales tax vendors

A practical invoice checklist

  • Your business or legal name, mailing address and contact details.
  • The customer’s name and billing address.
  • A unique invoice number, issue date and service date or period.
  • A clear description of work or goods, quantities, rates and line totals.
  • Subtotal, discounts, any applicable sales tax and total due, with currency.
  • Payment deadline, agreed terms and payment instructions.

This is a practical billing checklist, not a claim that federal law requires every listed field on every invoice. Keep invoices with supporting records so your income and expenses can be traced; an invoice alone is not proof that it was paid.

Source: IRS — What kind of records should I keep? · IRS — Publication 583: recordkeeping

Where sales tax applies, check the state’s required invoice details. New York’s separate tax disclosure is one example, not a rule we can generalize to all states.

Source: New York — Recordkeeping for sales tax vendors

EIN, SSN and Form W-9

As a privacy practice, keep your Social Security number off routine customer invoices. If a business tax identifier is needed and you have an appropriate EIN, use it rather than exposing your SSN. An EIN is not a universal mandatory invoice field.

Source: IRS — Identity protection tips (archived guidance) · IRS — Publication 583: recordkeeping

Form W-9 is a separate request for your correct name and taxpayer identification number. Its instructions allow a sole proprietor who has an EIN to use either that EIN or an SSN; a disregarded single-member LLC follows the owner’s TIN rules. Give a requested W-9 to the requester through a secure channel, not as a public invoice attachment.

Source: IRS — Form W-9 and instructions (March 2024) · IRS — Identity protection tips (archived guidance)

In Billero’s U.S. business profile, use the EIN field for an EIN or leave it blank. Do not enter an SSN: the value can appear on your customer PDFs.

Sales tax depends on where you do business

A physical or economic connection to a state, called nexus, may create state and local sales-tax collection duties. Nexus standards differ. Check the relevant state authority for registration, whether the product or service is taxable, and the applicable location and rate.

Source: SBA — Grow your business: taxes for online sales

Billero lets you enter a sales-tax rate and calculates from your invoice settings. It does not determine nexus, decide taxability, look up the correct jurisdiction rate, register you or file sales-tax returns.

W-9 and 1099 are separate from your invoice

A business customer may request Form W-9 and may need to report contractor payments on Form 1099-NEC when the current reporting rules apply. Thresholds and exceptions must be checked for the payment year. Issuing an invoice does not replace either form, and Billero does not prepare or file them.

Source: IRS — Forms and taxes for independent contractors

How to do it in Billero

  • Choose United States in the business profile; check your name, address and optional EIN.
  • Select the client and add the service, amounts and payment due date.
  • Enter your own sales-tax rate when applicable; check the resulting total.
  • Create and share an English PDF in Letter format. You can use the app interface in English or U.S. Spanish.
  • Add bank details or a QR code for your own payment link. You can also create an estimate and convert it into an invoice.

What Billero does not do

Billero 1.8.1 creates PDFs. It does not certify legal compliance, determine sales-tax obligations or send tax forms to the IRS. The U.S. document is in English even when the app interface is Spanish; it does not use Slovenia’s UPN or Europe’s SEPA bank-transfer QR. A payment-link QR opens your chosen payment provider.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a mandatory federal invoice format for sole proprietors?

There is no single federal template for ordinary domestic service invoices. IRS recordkeeping guidance allows a system that clearly records income and expenses. State, industry or contract requirements may add obligations.

Source: IRS — What kind of records should I keep?
Do I have to put my SSN on a customer invoice?

Do not put your SSN into Billero’s EIN field. Keep routine invoices separate from tax-ID requests. If a customer requests Form W-9, supply the appropriate TIN according to the IRS instructions through a secure channel.

Source: IRS — Form W-9 and instructions (March 2024) · IRS — Identity protection tips (archived guidance)
Does Billero work out my sales-tax rate or nexus?

No. You determine the applicable tax treatment and enter the rate. Nexus and state or local requirements need checking with the relevant tax authority.

Source: SBA — Grow your business: taxes for online sales
Does a Billero invoice replace a W-9 or 1099?

No. W-9 identifies the payee for tax purposes; 1099-NEC reports qualifying payments. Billero does not prepare or file either form.

Source: IRS — Forms and taxes for independent contractors
Can I use Spanish and send an English invoice?

Yes. With United States selected as the issuer country, Billero produces an English PDF in Letter format while the app interface can be U.S. Spanish.
